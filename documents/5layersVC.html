VC Operations — The 5-Layer Model

A practical model for understanding how a venture firm moves from raw evidence to investment pipelines, while preserving traceability and keeping business decisions human-controlled.

Evidence-first Business logic begins at Layer 3 Pipelines are executive-facing Campaigns implement pipelines

1. Executive Summary

This model describes how a VC firm operates across five connected layers of records.

At the bottom are immutable evidence records. These support notes, interpretations, and structured operational facts. Once those facts are used to evaluate investable things inside a business process, the system enters business logic.

The highest-level business logic is expressed as pipelines. Pipelines are what operators and executives use to talk about how leads become investments and how investments are later monitored for returns.

Processes

Networking, Qualifying/Qualification, Committing/Commitment, and Monitoring & Reporting are activities.

They are things the firm does.

States / Concepts

Leads, Opportunity, Candidate, Investment, and ROI/Returns are states or business concepts.

They describe what stage something is in.

3. Abstract Pipeline

The abstract pipeline is the master lifecycle used by the firm to reason about investable things from first contact to returns.

It is intentionally generic. Different asset classes or investment approaches can specialize it in different ways, but they all roughly follow the same shape shown above.

Stage meanings

Lead = something on the firm’s radar before it has been promoted into a more serious business state.
Networking = the ongoing relationship-building, information-gathering, trust-building, and context-building process around leads.
Opportunity = a lead that became interesting because a meaningful signal was spotted.
Qualification = the process of checking whether the opportunity meets the criteria to move forward.
Candidate = something that passed qualification and is now serious enough that the firm is trying to make the deal work.
Commitment = the process of firming up the investment, often relying on both internal and external signals.
Investment = the successful target state reached when a lead makes it all the way through.
Monitoring & Reporting = the ongoing process of tracking what happened after investment.
ROI = the realized or measured return to the firm and its stakeholders.
A lead does not need a special rejected state in the base model. It can simply stop being promoted. Additional states like watchlist, revisit later, or follow up in 6 months are meaningful extensions, but they are not required to define the core abstract pipeline.

4. Pipeline vs Campaign

Pipeline

A pipeline is a reusable business process definition. It describes how a class of investments should move through the abstract pipeline.

Example: the venture-backed company pipeline.

Campaign

A campaign is an implemented pipeline. It is a real operational instance of a pipeline, often tied to a specific cohort, theme, event, thesis, or sourcing effort.

Example: a pitch competition cohort where 20 companies are evaluated using the venture-backed company pipeline.

Pipeline = reusable business process definition Campaign = implemented instance of that pipeline

5. Shared Lead Universe

Leads are shared across the firm. They are not owned by just one pipeline.

A pipeline can intake many kinds of investable things. Sometimes the input is a single thing. Sometimes the input is a combination.

Single-input examples

  • a company
  • a token
  • a project
  • a product
  • a person

Combination-input examples

  • founder + project
  • team + company
  • token + ecosystem thesis
  • person + product idea

This nuance matters most in pipelines like incubation. Incubation may evaluate:

  • a project looking for founders
  • founders looking for projects
  • premade founder-project matches

So when this document talks about a lead moving through a pipeline, the lead should be understood as an investable subject. That investable subject may be:

  • a single entity, or
  • a structured combination of entities
One investable subject can appear in more than one pipeline or campaign at the same time. That is valid.

6. The 5 Layers

Layer 0 — Evidence

What it is: immutable source material.

Why it matters: this is the raw basis for all interpretation and later decision-making.

  • meeting recordings
  • emails
  • messages
  • documents
  • audio / video
  • images
  • archived web pages
  • social posts, comments, chat logs

Important: interactions are a major subset of evidence, but not all evidence is interaction-based.

Layer 1 — Extractions & Interpretations

What it is: authored notes or generated outputs derived from evidence.

  • meeting notes
  • research notes
  • summaries
  • transcriptions
  • OCR
  • observations
  • insights

This layer is observer-dependent. Different people or models may produce different interpretations from the same evidence.

Layer 2 — Operational Facts & Canonical Statements

What it is: structured, machine-readable statements built from Layer 1 material.

  • normalized facts
  • current believed conclusions
  • business insights
  • tentative or disputed statements
  • approved or signed-off statements

This is the final upstream layer before business logic begins.

Layer 3 — Pipeline Stage Records

What it is: a record of an investable subject being evaluated at one process stage.

This is where business logic begins.

  • checklist-driven
  • human-triggered
  • can prefill from Layer 2
  • supports proceed / no proceed decisions
  • often one table per stage or process node

Reevaluation model: freeze old row, generate a new row, and visually flag potential staleness when new data arrives.

Layer 4 — Pipelines

What it is: the highest-level business logic used to organize and communicate how the firm invests.

  • executive-facing
  • human-defined
  • composed of stage records
  • implemented as campaigns

This is where the business talks in terms like deal flow, token investing, incubation flow, and so on.

7. The Three Identified Pipelines

1. Venture-Backed Company Pipeline

Used for traditional venture investing into companies.

Investment state: a venture-backed company.

Typical specialization: founder diligence, company diligence, product/GTM diligence, technical diligence, capital commitment.

2. Liquid Token Pipeline

Used for direct token investing and speculation.

Investment state: an invested token position.

Typical specialization: fundamental analysis, technical analysis, allocation decision, trade execution, price watch.

3. Incubation Pipeline

Used for shaping projects, matching founders, and maturing opportunities into venture-backable companies.

Investment state: often realized later by promotion into the venture-backed company pipeline once there is traction.

Typical specialization: thesis, gaps, product idea, founder matching, POC, MVP, traction.

8. Cross-Pipeline Rules

  • A lead can enter zero, one, or multiple pipelines.
  • The same investable subject can reappear later, such as a company in a later round or a token under renewed analysis.
  • A pipeline may accept a single entity or a combination of entities, depending on its rules.
  • Monitoring & Reporting is a universal abstract stage, even if some concrete pipelines skip or simplify it.
  • Commitment can depend on external signals as well as internal judgment.

9. Worked Examples

Example A — Pitch Competition Cohort

A VC runs a pitch competition for 20 startups.

  • Pipeline used: Venture-Backed Company Pipeline
  • Campaign: Spring 2026 Pitch Competition Cohort
  • Shared lead universe: the 20 companies first appear as leads
  • Opportunity: some stand out based on signals
  • Qualification: the firm evaluates team, market, product, and technical fit
  • Candidate: the strongest ones become serious candidates
  • Commitment: negotiation, diligence completion, internal approval, external alignment
  • Investment: selected companies become venture-backed investments

Example B — Token on the Radar

A token appears on the firm's radar due to a market narrative and on-chain activity.

  • Lead: the token is noticed
  • Networking equivalent: market watching, research, conversations, ecosystem signals
  • Opportunity: enough signal appears to justify analysis
  • Qualification: fundamental and technical review
  • Candidate: a serious trade or allocation case exists
  • Commitment: the firm finalizes position sizing and entry
  • Investment: the token position is taken
  • Monitoring: price watch and position review

Example C — Incubation to Venture Pipeline

A thesis area is identified and one of several possible investable subjects becomes promising.

  • Possible lead forms: project, founder, product idea, or founder + project match
  • Opportunity: the fit looks promising
  • Qualification: founder-project fit and feasibility are assessed
  • Candidate: the subject is strong enough to move into serious build mode
  • Commitment: founder alignment, MVP push, and early support
  • Result: once the MVP is out and there is traction, it can be promoted into the Venture-Backed Company Pipeline

10. The Mental Model in One View

Shared lead universe ↓ Networking and evidence collection ↓ Layer 0: Evidence ↓ Layer 1: Extractions & Interpretations ↓ Layer 2: Operational Facts & Canonical Statements ↓ Layer 3: Stage records for concrete business decisions ↓ Layer 4: Pipelines ↓ Campaigns implement pipelines in the real world ↓ Investments are monitored for ROI

11. Entity Model

The system needs a vocabulary for what kinds of things can exist in the shared lead universe and what kinds of things can be evaluated inside pipelines.

The simplest useful distinction is between single entities and combination subjects.

Single entities

  • Person — an individual human
  • Company — a legal or operating business entity
  • Organization — a broader coordinated group, which may or may not be a company
  • Project — an initiative, venture concept, or developing effort that may not yet be a company
  • Product — a specific product, offering, or build effort
  • Token — a crypto asset or tokenized instrument

Combination subjects

  • Founder + Project
  • Team + Company
  • Person + Product idea
  • Token + ecosystem thesis

These are not just notes. They can be valid pipeline inputs in their own right.

Why this matters

A pipeline should not be forced to assume its input is always a company. Some pipelines will evaluate companies directly. Others, especially incubation, may evaluate combinations like founder + project fit.

Term Working meaning Why it matters operationally
Person An individual human actor Can be part of sourcing, diligence, founder matching, or leadership evaluation
Company A business entity that can receive investment or operate commercially Common input to venture pipelines
Organization A broader group that may not map cleanly to one company Useful when the real-world subject is coordinated but not yet packaged as a single company
Project An emerging initiative that may later become a company Especially important in incubation
Product A specific product or offering, whether or not the company structure is settled Can matter for early evaluation and build-stage logic
Token A crypto asset or tokenized investment subject Direct input to liquid token pipelines
Combination subject A structured pairing or grouping of entities evaluated together Lets the system represent cases like founder + project fit instead of flattening them too early
The most important idea is not the label itself. The important idea is that the pipeline input can be either a single entity or a structured combination.
